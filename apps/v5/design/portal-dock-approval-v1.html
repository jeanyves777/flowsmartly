What this is, and what it is not

The dock approval affordance, authored in PORTAL-SHELL-SPEC.md §4

The approved shell mockup (portal-shell-v1.html) has a dock with a field, a mic, a send and “Open panel”. It has no approve affordance — that is C-1. §4 authors one; this page renders it so it can be looked at before it is signed off.

This is a static mock. It creates no route, no contract and no client model. Every value below is a hard-coded fixture standing in for a recorded backend answer. Nothing here computes authority, eligibility, cost or approval state — that is FE15a, and it is the rule the real component must also keep.
Read the dashes. Every slot in the dock is outlined by where its value would come from. Green = the field exists in PendingWait today. Amber = it does not exist and the frontend must not invent it — that is C-2, and the amber slots are precisely the contract you are being asked to require.

§4.3, the three rules this demonstrates. No optimistic removal — on 2xx the row stays in submitting until the refreshed projection stops listing that waitId; press “Projection settles” to see the row leave. An empty list is an assertion only the projection may make (ADR 0005). Refusals are rendered, not swallowed — each refusal renders Problem.title, never prose parsed out of detail. approval_stale must not read as an error — it is the system working, and it is styled accordingly.
§4.4 — cost is mandatory before Approve is enabled. Untick “carries a cost ceiling”: Approve disables and only Review renders, with “Open to see what this costs”. Approving against an unknown ceiling is exactly what doc 00 §A3’s cost ceiling exists to prevent, and holdApproval would refuse it later with cost_exceeds_approval anyway.

§4.3 · the branch you asked to see

Approve / Reject — and why Reject is not in the dock

§4.3 is explicit: “Reject is not in the dock.” Two controls fit on a one-line strip; it carries the affirmative one and Review. Rejecting lives in the panel’s ApprovalCard, where the reason can be captured — a one-line strip is not the place to record why something was refused.

This contradicts the wording of your C-1 decision, which says “the dock may show Approve / Reject”. §4 as written puts only Approve in the dock. Both are shown below so you can pick one — they are not the same product decision, and the spec cannot record both.

A · §4 as authored — Approve only in the dock

Rejecting requires opening the panel. One extra tap to refuse, and the refusal always carries a reason.

B · your C-1 wording — Approve and Reject in the dock

Refusal is one tap — but no reason is captured, and at 390px the row cannot hold label + cost + expiry + two buttons without dropping something §4.5 says never drops.
