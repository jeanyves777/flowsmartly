Where the decisions stand

Your reference settles most of them. Two are genuinely open.

Where does Flow.AI live?

All three, switchable from its own header — plus collapsed, so it is reachable without occupying anything. Each mode has a job: sidebar for working alongside it, floating for a quick question without losing your place, full page when the assistant is the task.

Sidebar, floating, full page, collapsed Settled

One data-chat switch drives all four; the content is written once.

Which mode does a new account open in?

This is the real question behind the switcher. Full page says “this product is an assistant that happens to have a dashboard”. Sidebar says “this is a dashboard with help attached”. It sets the expectation on day one, and most people never change a default.

Full page on first run, sidebar after Recommended

The empty dashboard is the worst first screen a new account can get; Flow.AI asking what you want is the best. Once there is data to look at, it steps aside.

Sidebar always

Consistent, and it makes the assistant feel like a helper rather than the product.

How much is Flow.AI allowed to do without asking?

Still the one that matters most. The public site promises “human-approved by default”, and the Approvals card here holds three items — but a business that has to approve forty things a day will turn the assistant off. Where the line sits decides how the whole product feels.

Rules once, then it runs Recommended

Approve a kind of action once — “always answer the phone”, “never spend over $50/day” — and anything outside it lands in Approvals.

Every action, every time

Safest, and unusable by week two.

Do costs appear before you commit?

Kept from v1 and folded into your layout rather than added as a separate meter: every recommendation and every approval carries what it costs, in credits or in money. The rail keeps the refer-and-earn card you had instead of a balance.

Cost on the item, not a meter Settled

“9 credits”, “$600 committed”, “spends $18/day more” — next to the button that spends it.
