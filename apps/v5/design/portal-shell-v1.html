Why the shell is shaped this way

Fourteen products is too many to put in a flat list, and a small-business owner does not think in products — they think in “what needs me today”. So Today is the home screen and everything else is filed under what it is for. Flow.AI is not a page you visit; it sits at the bottom of every screen and opens a workspace on the right when you ask it for something, so you never lose the thing you were looking at.

Credits are on screen permanently, because the product is metered and a surprise bill is the fastest way to lose a small business. Every action Flow.AI proposes carries what it costs before you approve it.

Five decisions before this gets built

Each of these changes what the shell is, not how it looks. The recommendation is marked, but the call is yours.

Does the rail show everything, or hide the studios behind a hub?

The mock shows every product, grouped. It is honest about how much you get, but it is fifteen items and it will only grow.

Grouped rail Built above

Everything reachable in one click. Costs vertical space and gets long.

Four items plus a hub

Today, Make, People, Numbers. “Make” opens the studio grid. Calmer, one click deeper.

How much is Flow.AI allowed to do without asking?

The public site promises “human-approved by default”. The mock honours that literally — but the “Ran without you” card already shows four things that went out on their own, under rules set once. Those two ideas need a boundary drawn between them.

Rules once, then it runs Recommended

You approve a kind of action once (“always answer the phone”), and anything outside it waits. Matches how the marketing reads.

Every action, every time

Safest, and unusable by week two — nobody approves 40 items a day.

Is Today a dashboard or a queue?

The mock leads with what needs you and puts the numbers above it. The alternative leads with the numbers, which is what most tools do — and what most owners ignore.

A queue with numbers on top Built above

You arrive, clear three things, leave. The metrics are context, not the job.

A dashboard you read

Familiar, and it makes the product feel like a report rather than an operator.

Do credits stay on screen?

The rail carries a live balance and every proposed action carries its cost. That is honest, and it also puts a price tag in the corner of your eye all day.

Always visible Built above

No surprise bills, and the cost of a choice is next to the choice.

Only when it matters

Show it at the point of spend and when the balance runs low. Less noise, more risk of a shock.

On a phone, is it the same product or a smaller one?

The phone frame shows four tabs and the approval queue — no studios. Building a whole video editor for a 390pt screen is a large amount of work for something few people will do standing up.

Approve and ask, not make Recommended

Phone handles the queue, the numbers and Flow.AI. Studios are desktop.

Everything, everywhere

Full parity. Roughly doubles the build and most studios will be cramped.
